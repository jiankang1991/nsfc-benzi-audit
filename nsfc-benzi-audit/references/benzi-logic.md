# Core Logic And Finding Decisions

This is the shared decision layer for application diagnosis. Domain references add relevant questions; they do not turn examples, headings or publication labels into universal requirements. Writing preferences live in [writing-advice.md](writing-advice.md); claim-specific burdens in [research-claims.md](research-claims.md); official applicability in [current-rules.md](current-rules.md).

## Establish What Is Being Claimed

Extract these elements wherever the author puts them; separate headings are not necessary:

| Element | Question to resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Object and conditions | What quantity, population, system or phenomenon is studied, under which conditions? |
| Scientific question | What relation, property, mechanism, model, method or evaluation principle remains to be understood? |
| Target result | What knowledge, bound, criterion, explanation, dataset or method would answer it? |
| Route | What work would produce that result, and which assumptions does it use? |
| Evidence and validation | What theory, observations, comparisons or tests could support or refute the claim? |
| Contribution and significance | What changes relative to relevant existing knowledge, and why does that change matter? |

Interpret method, prediction and engineering language through the proposition actually made. A statistical-method question can concern validity, calibration, generalization, estimation or optimization without claiming a biological/physical mechanism. Model construction alone does not demonstrate novelty, but its vocabulary does not disqualify a scientific question.

Calibrate scope and resources to the category and career stage. Youth projects need credible ownership and capacity to start; larger projects need coherent integration and support for their scope. Neither project type prescribes a fixed count of tasks, questions, papers, team members or completed preliminary results. A justified change of direction may rely on transferable methods and available resources.

## COV — Coverage And Cross-Section Consistency

**Applies when:** the supplied draft states questions, tasks or outcomes. **Evidence:** the actual question, task, target and test passages. **Exception:** partial inputs limit a missing-coverage conclusion. **Basis:** logical support between an explicit promise and its plan, not an official form rule.

| Question/claim | Task(s) and role | Sought result | Supporting theory/test | Location or unresolved gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Use actual labels or short names | Modeling, solving, evidence, validation, integration; may be shared | | | |

- Evaluate many-to-many coverage. Unequal list lengths, prose paragraphs, shared validation and one task supporting several questions are normal possibilities.
- Trace an important condition from its claim to the model and validation. If the title promises cross-region performance but only within-region evaluation exists, locate both ends of that mismatch before reporting it.
- Derive dependencies from actual inputs and outputs. Parallel studies can jointly answer complementary questions; feedback and same-year staged delivery can be valid. A linear mechanism→method→validation chain is one pattern, not a requirement.
- A missing dedicated heading, diagram or locally repeated explanation does not establish missing substance. Search the entire supplied source, including resource notes, appendices and original figures relevant to the claim.
- A genuinely unanswered question or unsupported outcome is one issue, even if it also appears as weak rationale, missing validation and an inconsistent innovation statement.

## EVD — Evidence, Readiness And Resource Support

**Applies when:** feasibility, personal contribution, prior results or external facts support a proposed step. **Evidence:** the supplied source and its actual verification level. **Exceptions:** unseen attachments, unpublished work, adjacent expertise, shared resources and planned results. **Basis:** evidence sufficiency for the specific inference.

- Keep question provenance, applicant ability and resource access separate. Literature, theory, observation or demand may motivate a question; personal contribution and task-specific resources establish readiness.
- Accept relevant proofs, conference/journal work, code, instruments, pilot data and resource access in their actual scope. No universal first-author SCI, pre-experiment, matching-paper-per-task or completed-work percentage is required.
- Separate completed components from proposed advances. A working solver does not establish that a new identifiability result or new validation setting has already been solved.
- Match critical needs to available sources, including shared equipment, existing data and platform allocations. A task need not incur its own new expense. Scheduling requires usable prerequisites, not separate calendar years for every dependency.
- Distinguish an author's statement, an inspected artifact and an externally verified fact. A contribution summary can clarify the claim without verifying the underlying method. A claimed permission with no supplied attachment is not a demonstrated lack of permission.
- For incomplete inputs, report which conclusion cannot be reached and what material would resolve it. Do not infer fabrication, noncompliance or incapacity from an unexamined source.

## Check Before Issuing A Finding

Keep a short working record for consequential candidates: `claim/location → applicable criterion → supporting and counterevidence → remaining gap → impact → smallest repair`. This can stay in working notes; it is not another form the applicant must fill out.

Before a 必改 or equivalent strong instruction:

1. **Applicability:** identify the actual claim and criterion. For a rule violation, verify year/category/stage and official text. A writing preference cannot justify a substantive defect.
2. **Countercheck:** look for the strongest explanation elsewhere in the supplied draft. Check original figures when relevant. Consider whether a narrower stated scope, planned deliverable or alternative evidence resolves the concern.
3. **Evidence boundary:** distinguish a contradiction visible in the material from a document or external fact not examined. Record residual uncertainty even when potential impact is high.
4. **Consequence and repair:** say what claimed conclusion or critical step fails and what smallest change would repair it. Claim narrowing, an existing-evidence reference or clarification may suffice; do not automatically add experiments or research tasks.

This is a focused check on consequential findings, not a mandatory extra agent or repeated whole-document review. If a specific reference conflicts with this decision layer, do not use the conflict to justify a strong opinion. Apply verified official requirements where relevant; otherwise use the narrower supported claim boundary or mark the criterion unresolved. Maintainers should repair the conflicting source, not add another overriding exception elsewhere.

| Result after countercheck | Appropriate disposition |
| --- | --- |
| Explicit claim has a demonstrated consequential contradiction or no supporting task within the examined complete scope | 必改: cite the promise and the failing support, with a concrete consequence |
| Important concern depends on an unseen attachment, unresolved external source or ambiguous premise | 待核实 / 需补材料: state potential impact conditionally; do not assert a violation |
| Current wording obscures an otherwise supported relation | 建议澄清: identify the relation and where its evidence already exists |
| Only style, preferred structure, counts or an inapplicable example differs | Optional polish if useful and requested; otherwise omit |
| Counterevidence resolves the candidate | Drop it; if it was a previous finding, close or withdraw it explicitly |

## Finding Identity And Report Economy

- Give each distinct root issue one stable ID. Record all affected locations there, explain it once, and cross-reference that ID from section or coverage notes. Distinct issues with different consequences/repairs should remain separate.
- Preserve old IDs in re-audits; document merges/splits and allocate unused IDs to new issues. Re-evaluate the old premise as well as the edit. Separate corrected plan text from verified implementation.
- Rank by consequence to the stated scientific claim, feasibility or verified requirement. Evidence certainty is a separate label or explanation, not a made-up probability or funding score.
- A complete review can find no substantive issues. Do not fill a preferred number of rows, invent gaps for balance, or replace resolved issues with unrelated demands.
- Review all requested material, but publish only useful distinct findings. A concise coverage record can establish what was checked; an empty checklist or repeated full matrices cannot add evidence.

For a long draft, maintain source/version locators and a compact question/task/evidence map as you read. Before closing, revisit only unresolved candidates and the related passages. Report unexamined source ranges honestly if access or extraction limits the review.
